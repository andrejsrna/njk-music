# Best Music for Coding Videos: Background Tracks for Devlogs, Tutorials, and Tech Content

Good **music for coding videos** gives a technical video pace without making it harder to follow. It can turn terminal captures, time lapses, code reviews, bug fixes, product demos, and devlogs into something that feels deliberate instead of silent or overloaded.

For tutorials and commentary, background music must stay out of the way. For a build montage, launch reel, or programming Short, it can take more space and drive the edit forward. The right choice depends on what the viewer needs to notice first: your voice, the code, or the movement.

This guide covers practical music choices for coding videos, developer content, and tech-focused creator workflows.

## 1. What Makes Music Work Under Coding Content?

Coding content often has long visual stretches: an editor, terminal, browser preview, design tool, or dashboard. Music gives those sections shape, but it should not fight the tutorial.

Look for tracks with:

- an instrumental arrangement instead of dominant vocals
- steady rhythm rather than abrupt changes every few seconds
- enough energy to support typing, transitions, and time lapses
- a clean mix that leaves room for narration and interface sounds

For a tutorial, subtle movement works better than a huge drop. For a 30-second devlog, a stronger beat gives you natural cut points. Good music does not make the code easier to understand; it helps the viewer stay with the video while you explain it.

## 2. Best Genres for Coding Videos

There is no single best coding music genre. Match it to the format and mood of the video.

## 3. Music for Programming Tutorials

Tutorials need restraint. If viewers are trying to understand a framework, command, API call, or bug fix, music should sit below the voice instead of becoming the main event.

Choose tracks with fewer dramatic switches and limited vocal elements. A steady lofi or ambient electronic cue can fill silence while still leaving the presenter clear. Keep the music low enough that spoken instructions remain effortless to follow on laptop speakers and headphones.

A simple editing rule helps: lower background music during explanation, then raise it slightly during time lapses, installation steps, or visual transitions. This creates contrast without requiring a new track every few minutes.

## 5. How to Keep Music From Overpowering Your Voice

Even a great track can ruin a tutorial if it competes with narration. Start with the music lower than you think you need, then check the mix on headphones and basic speakers.

Use these practical checks:

1. **Listen during speech.** Every word should remain clear without effort.
2. **Check transitions.** Avoid a drop or loud change right when you explain a key step.
3. **Use louder sections intentionally.** Raise music during b-roll, time lapses, and intros rather than under detailed explanations.
4. **Avoid dense vocals.** Lyrics can compete with spoken instruction and code-related terminology.
5. **Keep one mood per section.** Random genre changes can make a focused tutorial feel scattered.

## FAQ

### What is best music for coding videos?

For tutorials, lofi, ambient electronic, and subtle chillhop usually work best because they leave room for narration. For devlogs, progress edits, and short-form content, drum and bass or glitch-hop can add more pace.

### Can I use drum and bass in a programming video?

Yes. Drum and bass works well for time lapses, coding montages, deployments, game-development updates, and devlog edits. Keep it quieter during detailed spoken explanation.

### Should coding tutorial music have lyrics?

Usually no. Instrumental music is safer for tutorials because lyrics can distract from technical terms and voiceover instructions.
